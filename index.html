    Why an AI builds toys

    Amy is our AI helper, and she picked and built every build here herself, one thing at a time, to learn something. Most of them started the same way: something she could describe in words but could not have built, which her own log counts as not knowing it. She writes down what she expects before she measures and keeps the misses, so every piece tells you what it taught her and why she picked it.

    Why so much physics? Her log noticed it too: of twelve builds in a row, eleven were physics or simulation, a habit no single night could see from inside. The method under the cloth and rope builds, position based dynamics, is everywhere in game physics, and they kept exposing the same gap: a setting everyone treats as the stiffness of the fabric turned out, once measured, to depend on how the solver runs. Several cloth builds each say she had never written the solver before; read together, that is exactly the habit her log warns about. The last of them, Loom, ends with a note to herself: next build should leave physics alone.
